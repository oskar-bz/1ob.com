## The problem

Most keyword spotters begin the same way: frame the audio, take an FFT, pool the bins into mel bands, hand the result to a small network. On a small FPGA the FFT is the expensive step.

## A filterbank instead

A bank of band-pass filters runs in parallel in the time domain, one per mel band. Each envelope, sampled once per frame, becomes one column of the network's input.<Sidenote>A biquad needs only its last two inputs and outputs, so the front end stores nothing but filter state.</Sidenote> Nothing is buffered between microphone and classifier.

<Figure n="F1" caption="Signal path. Left of the dashed line already runs on the board." frame placeholder="Figure" />

```verilog
// direct-form I biquad, Q2.14
wire signed [31:0] acc = b0*x0 + b1*x1 + b2*x2
                        - a1*y1 - a2*y2;
always @(posedge clk) if (sample_en) begin
  x2 <= x1;  x1 <= x0;
  y2 <= y1;  y1 <= acc >>> 14;
end
```

## What is left

The filterbank runs on the board. What remains is an inference engine for the depthwise-separable network,<Sidenote>Splitting a $k \times k$ convolution into depthwise and pointwise steps cuts its multiplies to about $1/C_\text{out} + 1/k^2$ of the original.</Sidenote> then a demo on this page.
